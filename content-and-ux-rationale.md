# Roelofs & Rubens — Content & User Experience Rationale

*A guide to the content decisions, page structure, and user journey design behind the new website. Prepared for client review.*

---

## Why Redesign the Website?

The current WordPress + WooCommerce + Elementor site was built to do everything — browse, buy, manage accounts, process payments. But the reality of the business has moved on:

- **Seasonal and collection sales happen on Etsy.** That's where the shop infrastructure, payment processing, reviews (5.0 stars, 341 reviews), and customer trust already live. Duplicating that on the website creates maintenance overhead with little upside.
- **Exclusive decorations aren't sold online at all.** They're available through stockists — cathedrals, castles, museums. What customers need isn't a "buy" button, it's information about *where* to find them.
- **The website's job isn't to be a shop.** It's to be the brand's home — telling the story, showcasing the work, and directing people to the right place to buy.

The new site strips away the e-commerce complexity and refocuses on what the website uniquely does well: **brand storytelling, product discovery, and wayfinding.**

---

## Site-Wide Content Strategy

### The Two Audiences

Every page is designed with two distinct visitors in mind:

1. **Gift buyers and decoration collectors** — browsing for seasonal decorations, looking for something charming and handmade. These people need a quick, delightful path to the Etsy shop.
2. **Venue buyers and trade customers** — gift shop managers at cathedrals, museum retail buyers, wholesale enquirers. These people need to see the exclusive range, find stockist information, and get in touch.

The homepage makes this split explicit with the "Two ways to discover our decorations" section — two clear cards that immediately route each audience to the right place.

### Tone of Voice

Warm, personal, proudly handmade. Not slick or corporate. The copy feels like a conversation with the makers, not a brand manifesto. Roelofs & Rubens is a small family business with a genuine craft — the content lets that come through without overselling.

Key principles:
- **First person plural** ("we", "our") — feels personal, not distant
- **Honest about the process** — mentions trial and error, cracked pieces, kiln mishaps. This builds trust and authenticity.
- **No jargon** — "handmade ceramic decorations" not "artisanal ceramic objets"
- **Understated confidence** — the work speaks for itself

## Home Page — Content Decisions

### Hero Section

**Decision:** Editorial-style hero with a lifestyle image, not a product grid or slider.

**Why:** The hero's job is to set the mood, not to sell. A large, beautifully composed image of the decorations in context (hung from a peg rail, on a mantelpiece) immediately communicates "handmade, charming, worth owning." The heading — *"Handmade ceramic decorations, designed in Berkshire"* — is factual and grounding. It tells you exactly what this is and where it comes from. No marketing fluff.

### Two-Path Intro

**Decision:** Explicitly split the browsing experience into two routes — Etsy shop vs. venue exclusives.

**Why:** Without this, first-time visitors would be confused. "Can I buy these online? Where?" The two-path section answers that question immediately. The Etsy card gets the primary button (most visitors will be gift buyers), while the Exclusives card gets a secondary button — respecting the likely traffic split while still giving venue buyers a clear path.

### Seasonal Carousel

**Decision:** Horizontal scrolling carousel of category cards on a mist-blue background.

**Why:** The seasonal range is broad (Christmas, animals, flowers, hearts, etc.) and image-forward. A carousel lets visitors scan the range quickly without committing to a specific category. Each card links directly to the corresponding Etsy section — one click from homepage to shop. The mist-blue background creates visual breathing room and a sense of softness that matches the brand.

### Exclusive Decorations Feature Blocks

**Decision:** Two alternating feature blocks (image left/text right, then reversed) rather than a grid.

**Why:** The exclusive decorations are the premium, story-rich part of the business. They deserve more than a thumbnail grid. The alternating layout creates a narrative rhythm — you learn about the landmark collection, then about the handmade process. This builds desire and context before the visitor even reaches the exclusives page.

### Bespoke Teaser

**Decision:** A standalone call-to-action section between the main content and the Instagram grid.

**Why:** Bespoke commissions are how Roelofs & Rubens originally grew the exclusive side of the business. This section plants the seed for venue buyers or anyone who might want a custom decoration — "Looking for something bespoke?" It's positioned near the bottom of the homepage because it's a secondary action (most visitors are browsing, not commissioning), but it's visually distinct enough to catch attention.

### Instagram Grid

**Decision:** 8-image grid of Instagram posts with a direct link to the account.

**Why:** Instagram is the brand's most active social channel and the best place to see new designs, studio updates, and behind-the-scenes content. Embedding the grid on the homepage serves two purposes: it adds fresh, authentic imagery to the page, and it drives followers. The hover zoom effect makes it interactive without being distracting.

## About Page — Content Decisions

The About page went through the most significant restructuring. The original brief called for a straightforward brand storytelling page, but through iteration we developed a richer narrative that tells the full Roelofs & Rubens story.

### Page Structure & Flow

The page follows a deliberate narrative arc:

1. **Hero intro** — Sets the scene: who, when, what
2. **Meet the founders** — The human story (with video)
3. **Delft blue... with a twist** — The design origin story
4. **Made by hand** — The craft process
5. **From homegrown beginnings to our studio** — Growth and team
6. **Did somebody say... exclusive?** — The bespoke/exclusive story
7. **Where to find us** — Contact and location

This isn't random — it mirrors the actual journey of the business: *founding inspiration → learning the craft → growing the team → expanding into exclusives → here's where we are today.*

### Section-by-Section Rationale

#### 1. Hero Intro

> "Roelofs & Rubens has been creating handmade ceramic decorations since 2006. Founded by Dutch designer Piet van den Beuken and Monty Abram, the business has grown from a love of Delft blue pottery into a distinctive collection of seasonal decorations, gifts, bespoke commissions and venue exclusives."

**Why this works:** It packs the essential brand facts into two sentences — founding year, founders' names, Dutch heritage, product range. A visitor who reads nothing else on this page still gets the full picture.

#### 2. Meet the Founders (Video + Text)

**Decision:** Embed the brand video alongside the founders' introduction rather than having them in separate sections.

**Why:** The video and the text tell the same story — combining them avoids redundancy. The video sits in the left column of a feature block, with the text introduction on the right. This gives visitors the choice: watch or read. Having the video prominently placed also adds personality and warmth that text alone can't achieve.

**Content note:** The video is embedded from YouTube using privacy-enhanced mode (youtube-nocookie.com) to minimise tracking of site visitors.

#### 3. Delft Blue... With a Twist

> "What started with blue-and-white inspiration has grown into a world of colour, pattern and playful detail."

**Decision:** Combined with the founders section into one visual block (text left, image right — reversed layout).

**Why:** The Delft blue origin story is inseparable from the founding story. Splitting them into separate sections would create two short, thin-feeling blocks. Combined, they create a satisfying "here's who we are, here's what inspired us" narrative beat. The reversed layout (text left, image right) creates visual variety within the section.

#### 4. Made by Hand

**Decision:** Three-column process grid (Shape, Paint, Fire & finish) on a mist-blue background.

**Why:** Process transparency builds trust and justifies the price point. When a customer understands that every decoration is individually shaped from clay, hand-painted with fine brushwork, and carefully inspected after firing, £18 feels like remarkable value. The three-step format is deliberately simple — it doesn't overwhelm, but it demonstrates genuine craft.

**Content note:** The copy deliberately acknowledges imperfection: *"Cracked pieces, kiln mishaps, warped shapes and seconds all became part of the process."* This is intentional — it makes the brand feel human and honest, not polished and corporate. Real makers have kiln disasters. Saying so builds more trust than pretending everything is perfect.

#### 5. From Homegrown Beginnings to Our Studio

> "What began as a very homegrown operation has grown into a dedicated studio setup."

**Decision:** Simple feature block with team photo and text.

**Why:** This section bridges the gap between "two founders in a cottage" and "a business that supplies 100+ venues." The team photo with colourful umbrellas is deliberately informal and joyful — it shows the people behind the brand without feeling like a corporate headshot page. The copy acknowledges the team's helpers while keeping the handmade, personal feel.

#### 6. Did Somebody Say... Exclusive?

**Decision:** Converted from a feature block into a carousel showcase on a mist-blue background.

**Why:** This was a key content decision. Originally conceived as a simple text-and-image block about bespoke commissions, we realised it was an opportunity to visually showcase the exclusive range — the most impressive and distinctive part of the business. A carousel of actual exclusive decorations (Stonehenge, Tower Bridge, Big Ben, Buckingham Palace, Windsor Castle, King's Guard) is far more compelling than describing them in text.

The carousel reuses the same component as the homepage seasonal carousel, maintaining visual consistency. The CTA — "See Exclusive Collection" — links to the Exclusives catalogue page, creating a clear path for visitors who are intrigued.

**Why "exclusive" not "bespoke":** The section was renamed from "Did somebody say... bespoke?" to "Did somebody say... exclusive?" because the carousel showcases finished exclusive products, not the bespoke commissioning process. "Exclusive" better sets expectations for what the visitor will see when they click through.

#### 7. Where to Find Us

**Decision:** Feature block with contact details (left) and a Google Maps embed (right) on a white background.

**Why:** The About page is a natural place for contact information — visitors who've just read the brand story are most likely to want to get in touch. The side-by-side layout keeps the contact details prominent while the map provides geographic context (near Newbury, Berkshire, M4/A34 junction). This is especially useful for venue buyers or trade customers considering a visit.

Contact details include email and phone with a "Get in Touch" CTA that opens the email client directly — reducing friction for enquiries.

### Visual Rhythm of the About Page

The alternating backgrounds create a deliberate visual rhythm:

| Section | Background | Scallop Waves |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| Hero | Mist blue | Bottom only |
| Founders + Delft blue | White | — |
| Made by hand | Mist blue | Top + bottom |
| Homegrown to studio | White | — |
| Did somebody say... exclusive? | Mist blue | Top + bottom |
| Where to find us | White | — |

This blue-white-blue-white alternation creates breathing room between content-heavy sections and prevents the page from feeling like one long scroll of text. The scallop wave dividers at the edges of each blue section add a soft, handmade quality that reinforces the brand's ceramic craft identity.

## Collections Page — Content Decisions

**Decision:** Category grid with 1:1 aspect ratio image cards that link out to Etsy.

**Why:** The collections page is a wayfinding page, not a shopping page. Its job is to help visitors find the right category and get them to the Etsy shop as efficiently as possible. Large, square images make each category visually identifiable. One click on a card takes the visitor directly to the filtered Etsy section.

No product-level detail is needed on-site because Etsy handles all of that — product images, descriptions, pricing, reviews, cart, and checkout. Duplicating that information would create a maintenance burden with no benefit.

---

## Exclusives Page — Content Decisions

**Decision:** Category grid with a longer description explaining that these products are stockist-only.

**Why:** This is the most important piece of expectation-setting on the site. Unlike the collections page (which links to Etsy for purchasing), the exclusives page leads to products that **cannot be bought online.** The description makes this clear upfront: *"These pieces are not sold directly online. Use the catalogue to explore the range and find where to buy each design."*

The italic note — *"Available from participating stockists"* — reinforces this at a glance for visitors who might skim past the paragraph.

---

## Stockists Page — Content Decisions

**Decision:** Map placeholder + featured stockist cards on a mist-blue background.

**Why:** The stockists page exists to answer one question: "Where can I find these decorations in person?" The map (once populated) will be the primary tool for this. The featured stockist cards below highlight the most prestigious venues — Canterbury Cathedral, Tower of London, Hampton Court Palace, Edinburgh Castle, Stonehenge, Windsor Castle — which also serves as social proof. If these iconic venues stock Roelofs & Rubens, the quality speaks for itself.

---

## Wholesale Page — Content Decisions

**Decision:** Three simple, focused sections — enquiry, login, trade fairs.

**Why:** Wholesale visitors know what they want. They're either:
1. **New** — "Do you wholesale?" → Yes, here's how to get in touch
2. **Existing** — "I have an account" → Here's the login
3. **Exploring** — "Where can I see you in person?" → Here are the trade fairs

The page doesn't try to sell — it answers questions and provides clear next steps. The mist-blue background on the login section visually separates the three blocks.

## Design Motifs & Their Purpose

### Scallop Wave Dividers

The soft, undulating wave shapes between sections are the site's most distinctive design element. They serve multiple purposes:

- **Brand reinforcement** — they evoke the seaside collection and the organic, handmade quality of ceramics
- **Visual softening** — they prevent harsh colour transitions between mist-blue and white sections
- **Craft identity** — in a world of sharp-edged, geometric web design, the curves feel warm and artisanal

### Decorative Accent Lines

A subtle gradient line appears beneath every major heading. It's a nod to the fine brushwork and decorative detail in the ceramics themselves — a small but consistent detail that ties the digital experience to the physical product.

### Card Elevation & Hover Effects

Every interactive card (category, stockist, process step, carousel) shares the same hover behaviour — a gentle upward lift with a deepening shadow. This creates a tactile, physical feel that's appropriate for a brand whose products are tangible, handmade objects meant to be held and hung.

## User Journeys

### Journey 1: Gift Buyer Looking for Christmas Decorations

1. Lands on **Home** → sees hero image, understands this is handmade ceramics
2. Sees **"Two ways to discover"** → clicks "Browse on Etsy"
3. *Alternatively:* scrolls to **Seasonal carousel** → clicks "Christmas" card
4. Arrives at **Etsy shop** filtered to Christmas section → browses and buys

**Key insight:** This visitor should be able to go from homepage to Etsy in **two clicks maximum.** The site's job is to create desire and provide a frictionless handoff.

### Journey 2: Museum Gift Shop Buyer Exploring Exclusives

1. Lands on **Home** → sees "Venue Exclusives" path card → clicks "Explore Exclusives"
2. Arrives at **Exclusives** catalogue → browses categories (Cathedrals, Museums, etc.)
3. Finds relevant products → sees "Available from participating stockists"
4. Visits **Stockists** page → finds nearest venue on map
5. *Or:* visits **Wholesale** page → submits enquiry to stock the products

**Key insight:** This visitor needs to understand that exclusives aren't sold online — and needs a clear path to either find existing stockists or become one.

### Journey 3: First-Time Visitor Who's Never Heard of R&R

1. Lands on **Home** → hero image and copy establish: handmade, Berkshire, since 2006
2. Scrolls through seasonal carousel → gets a feel for the product range
3. Sees exclusive decorations feature blocks → intrigued by landmark collection
4. Sees bespoke teaser → "Oh, they do custom work too"
5. Visits **About** page → watches founder video, reads the story
6. Follows on **Instagram** → stays connected for new designs

**Key insight:** The homepage should create a complete first impression without requiring the visitor to click anywhere. The scroll tells a story: *here's what we make → here's where to get it → here's what makes us special → here's how to stay in touch.*

### Journey 4: Trade Customer at a Trade Fair

1. Met R&R at the Museums & Heritage Show
2. Visits **roelofsrubens.co.uk** from a business card
3. Goes straight to **Wholesale** page → "Do we wholesale? Yes."
4. Clicks **"Get in Touch"** → sends an enquiry
5. *Later:* returns to **Wholesale** → clicks **"Log In"** to access their account

**Key insight:** Trade customers need efficiency, not storytelling. The wholesale page is deliberately minimal — answer the question, provide the action, done.

## Content That Was Removed (and Why)

| Removed | Reason |
|---------|--------|
| On-site product pages for seasonal items | Etsy handles this better — product images, descriptions, pricing, reviews, cart, checkout, and payment processing are all already there |
| Shopping cart and checkout flow | No longer needed — seasonal sales go through Etsy, exclusives are bought at stockists |
| Customer account / order history | Etsy manages this for seasonal buyers; wholesale accounts are handled separately |
| WooCommerce product management | Replaced by Etsy for seasonal, Odoo for exclusives catalogue |
| Multiple separate stockist maps | Consolidated into one page with a single interactive map |
| Shipping calculator | Etsy handles shipping for all direct-to-consumer sales |

**What this achieves:** The site goes from 200+ pages (products, cart, checkout, account, etc.) to approximately 10 focused pages. Faster to load, easier to maintain, clearer for visitors.
